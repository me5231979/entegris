02/07The situation

Good news at Materials Solutions

The plant issues are resolved. You finally have time to meet with Jessica and the Customer Services team.

Jessica has led this work for months. Next week, the team presents its recommendations to stakeholders.

They feel confident. They still want your input on the business case, the messaging, and the strategy.

You are the leader. How do you proceed?

03/07Your choice

Choose a response

Pick the option closest to what you would do.

Option A · Reclaims ownership

Step back in

Taking over sends a clear message: when it counts, this is not your work. Jessica led for months, and the team loses the visible moment they earned. Confidence and credibility take the hit.

Option B · Leaves the team alone

Send an email

A vague message offers no guidance or coaching to a team that asked for support. They are still unsure what stakeholders expect, and their anxiety rises before a high-visibility moment.

Option C · Reinforces ownership

Meet and listen first

You show up, you coach, and you resist the urge to take control when pressure rises. Jessica keeps clear ownership and real decision-making authority. The team feels empowered to move on.

04/07Debrief

What each option tells the team

  • A · Step back inReclaims the work. The team learns that ownership ends when the stakes go up.
  • B · Send an emailOffers trust without support. The team is left guessing.
  • C · Meet and listen firstReinforces ownership. You provide support and coaching while Jessica keeps the lead.

05/07From the Playbook

Empowerment

More than assigning tasks. Clear ownership, decision authority, and confidence.

  • Ownership changes workWhen people own the outcome, they think strategically, anticipate risks, and take responsibility for results.
  • It speeds workFewer bottlenecks. Faster decisions. Stronger accountability across the team.
  • It builds capabilityLeading part of a project builds judgment, confidence, and presence.
  • Your roleSet guardrails. Coach up front. Resist the urge to step in under pressure.
  • BalanceHold people accountable and build their belief at the same time.
Playbook

Reinforcing ownership in visible, high-stakes moments creates a culture where initiative thrives and capability grows with every stretch opportunity.

06/07Reflect

Am I truly empowering my team?

Empowerment is not something we declare. It is something our teams experience. Take a few minutes with these questions. Your notes stay on this device.

  1. If progress slows, decisions stall, or stakeholders wait for you, that is a signal.

  2. Do you step in when the stakes rise? Answer questions directed to your team? Or stay behind them and let them lead?

  3. Are they proposing solutions instead of escalating problems? Owning stakeholder conversations? Growing in judgment?
